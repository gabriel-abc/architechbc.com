Services

Six ways I get brought in.

Most engagements start as one of these and grow into another. All of them are scoped against a written Statement of Work before any code is written.

01

Quote-to-cash architecture

The chain from a quote to money in the bank, built or repaired end to end: configuration and pricing, billing schedules, payment capture across more than one gateway, revenue recognition, and the handoff into accounting — including the reconciliation nobody notices until the numbers disagree. Approval rules included, because that is usually where quoting actually stalls.

  • CPQ
  • Billing
  • Revenue recognition
  • Approval rules
02

Integration engineering

Connecting Salesforce to the systems that actually run the business — billing platforms, operator APIs, payment providers, internal services. Built with real error handling, so failures surface loudly instead of disappearing into a debug log.

  • Apex callouts
  • Named credentials
  • Queueable / async
  • REST & gRPC-web
03

Portals & customer-facing applications

Applications for the people outside your company — customers, vendors, contractors, advisors — each with their own login, their own slice of the data and their own idea of what the system is for. Built on one model rather than three, so a change to the process doesn't have to be made three times.

  • Experience Cloud
  • Multi-persona access
  • Self-service
  • Sharing model
04

Replacing a system you've outgrown

Moving off a legacy platform, a competing product, or the spreadsheet that quietly became one. The work is mostly reading: establishing what the old system really does, including the parts nobody documented, before deciding what deserves to be carried across at all.

  • Data modelling
  • Migration
  • Reconciliation
  • Cutover
05

Automation audit & documentation

Find out what your org actually does. Every flow, trigger, validation rule and assignment rule traced, tested against live behaviour, and written up in language your admins and your auditors can both use.

  • Flow archaeology
  • Conflict detection
  • Written guides
  • Handover
06

Retained engineering

Ongoing senior capacity for teams that need an architect occasionally but can't justify one full-time. Deployment discipline included: targeted, test-gated releases, and a production org that is never a guess.

  • Monthly retainer
  • Release management
  • Code review
  • Escalation

Approach

How I work, stated plainly.

These are the commitments that tend to matter most to the people who hire me, so they're worth putting in writing before we ever speak.

01

You work with the engineer

Architech is one person by design. The person who scopes your project is the person who writes the code and the person who answers the phone when something breaks. Nothing is translated through an account manager or passed to a delivery team you've never met.

02

Nothing ships undocumented

Every engagement ends with written documentation of what was built and why. Consultants who leave behind undocumented systems create the exact problem I usually get hired to fix — so I refuse to be the next one.

03

Production is never a guess

Targeted, test-gated deployments only — specific components with their specific tests, never a whole directory pushed and hoped over. You always see the exact change before it reaches production.

04

I'll tell you when not to build

Sometimes the honest answer is that a feature isn't worth what it costs to maintain, or that a licence you already own solves it. One of my design documents states plainly that the build needs no custom Apex or pages at all; another recommends postponing a document-generation tool until the first phase had earned it. That costs me the most billable part of a project, and buys the kind of trust that keeps clients for years.

Selected work

What the projects actually look like.

Client names are withheld under the confidentiality terms of my standard agreement. Described by problem and shape instead — which is the part worth reading anyway.

Telecom · Billing

Multi-region billing integration

Flow-invocable Apex connecting Salesforce to an operator billing platform across seven regional endpoints. Every batch is validated completely before a single callout fires, so a bad row fails the batch instead of leaving half the records sent.

Enterprise · Org audit

Automation archaeology

A mature org where nobody remaining knew what the automation did. Traced every flow and rule against live behaviour, eliminated well over a hundred false positives from the initial scan, and delivered a twenty-three chapter guide the team still works from.

Financial operations

Revenue share & multi-currency

Revenue-share calculation and multi-currency reconciliation built on top of a managed accounting package, including the currency-conversion corrections that made the USD figures reconcile for the first time.

Corporate wellbeing · Platform

A booking platform, and the org behind it

A custom scheduling service — its own API, authorisation layer and notification workers — talking both ways to a Salesforce org built to match: orders into sessions into shifts, practitioner availability, time entries and post-session scoring. Bookings arriving from five instances of a third-party scheduling product land in the same model as everything else.

Compliance software · Quote-to-cash

Contracted revenue, recognised correctly

Configure-price-quote through billing schedules into revenue contracts and recognition, with an approval matrix in front of it and more than one payment gateway behind it. Cloud-marketplace orders land in the same pipeline as direct sales rather than in a spreadsheet beside it.

Real-estate media · Portal

Three portals, one model

A marketplace where photographers, processing staff and agents each sign in to the same system and see a different one. Orders fan out into work orders and line items, move through an upload-and-review pipeline, and come back as a finished product — with payment capture and order history in the place the work actually happens.

Legal · Practice management

Three products read, one model drawn

Matters, claims, damages, negotiations, time and expense entries and billing, modelled from scratch. The design work was mostly forensic: reading three competing practice management products to establish what the firm actually relied on, before deciding what deserved to be rebuilt at all.

Wealth management · Data integration

Portfolio data where the advisers already work

A sync between Salesforce and a portfolio accounting platform — households, financial accounts, holdings and balance history — with custom pages for the parts the standard integration wouldn't do, and a migration that merged the old records into the new model instead of leaving the firm running both.

I've spent my career inside other people's Salesforce orgs — usually the ones that have been running long enough to accumulate history. Layers of automation added by people who have since left. Managed packages nobody fully understands. Integrations that work right up until the day they quietly stop.

The work I'm best at is making those systems legible again, and then extending them without adding to the pile. The other half of the job is the opposite: drawing the object model before anything is built, so the org has a shape worth inheriting in the first place. Either way I spend as much time reading and drawing as writing new code, and I think that ratio is the reason the systems I build tend to outlast my involvement in them.

Architech has operated since 2017. I work with a small number of clients at a time, directly and under a standard master services agreement, which is the arrangement that produces the best work for everyone involved.
